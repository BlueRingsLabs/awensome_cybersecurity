Okay, to the point. When looking for a VPS you should pay attention to how long a given company has been on the market, those that have been around for a short time are not bad, but I would not put something that is supposed to be a professional service there, rather something for a home project or on a smaller scale. This does not change the fact that those that have been around for a long time will not disappear without a word. Read opinions, check the response of the community and customers. Test the speed of data transfer, on the test links of individual data centers, choose the ones closest to you or for your target recipients, and the quality of the connection from home or the place from which you will manage the server. Choose the right amount of RAM, disk capacity, number of processors and network bandwidth. The parameters can be easily selected by reading the documentation of the service you want to set up. It is known that file hosting, for example, requires a large disk space and maximum bandwidth and transfer and not necessary large amount of RAM or CPU, etc. Check the status and error pages, each provider has a public page with server status and a history of errors and downtime. If too many things are red and too often, it doesn’t bode well. It is also worth choosing VPS on KVM rather than OpenVZ. If you are a beginner, it does not mean much to you which one you choose, but when you get more experienced you will find it more useful to use KVM. OpenVZ is container based (paravirtualization) and KVM is full visualization (hypervisor based). In short, it’s all about efficiency and flexibility. For most modern applications, especially in production and commercial environments, KVM is the more versatile and flexible choice.
