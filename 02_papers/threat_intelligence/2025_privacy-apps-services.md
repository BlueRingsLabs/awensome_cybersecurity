# Privacy applications and services

There are those who shout to abandon Google (or whatever big tech company), others shout to abandon closed source software, others recommend not using solutions from large corporations, and you also hear not to use open source or projects created by enthusiasts because they are not secure or of lower quality. And the famous “if it is free, you are the product”. There are as many ideas as there are people. There is a grain of truth in everything. And I agree with most of it. But it’s best to have your own brain, reason logically and choose what’s best for you at any given time.

It is also worth choosing solutions that are secure and provide privacy in this crazy world we now live in. Our choices today, will also affect what we get in the future. If everyone chooses good solutions then the bad ones will die out. This is not only true of technology. If we all gave up junk food today, there wouldn’t be so much junk food on the shelves etc.

In my opinion, you should use whatever you want, just be aware of it (read the policy, check the manufacturer, read reviews). I will soon be writing an article about people’s awareness of the Internet and technology, or rather their lack of awareness, but today I would like to recommend interesting software and services that might be of interest to you as alternatives to those known and used by everyone. I am not writing this to criticise one or the other, nor to force anyone to do anything.

I would like each of you to think about your own technologies, tools and software that you use every day. It is important to be open to alternatives and willing to change. The world, and technology in particular, is changing every day. Just because you have always used the “free” version of [WinRar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lX_pF03vCSU) does not mean that you should continue to do so until you die. I personally check from time to time that what I am using is the best solution for what I want to achieve. I make sure that the software I’m using is still being developed, or that there is a new alternative on the market. I also check that the vendor of a solution I am using has not drastically changed its rules or violated any standards. Most people say ‘if it works, why change it’. If we did that, we’d probably still be in the Middle Ages. It’s hard to step out of your comfort zone and try something new. Mostly because it requires action, such as searching for information, analysing it, comparing it, drawing conclusions and so on (a lot of work!). I assure you, it is worth it. In my career I have used paid solutions, free solutions, solutions from large companies and from individual developers. My choices have been based on my financial status, my level of knowledge and the requirements of third parties such as schools, government or other institutions. Currently, most of the things I use are open source, with an emphasis on privacy where possible, and only what is in constant development. I also use paid solutions, due to the fact that my financial situation is better than when I was a teenager, I can afford paid solutions. When I choose paid solutions, I also do my research, where one element, but not the only one, is privacy, professionalism and a human approach to the customer. I also try to support free projects and their developers. I do this financially, or by helping to solve problems or report bugs, or by doing a translation into my native language, or just by being an active member of the community around a given solution.

An example of paid solutions. I have been editing video content for my own home library as a hobby for a long time. I started with programs such as Magix Movie Studio, Vegas Pro, Corel Video Studio, etc. This was back in the day and I paid for each of them, more or less. After a while, I edited so little that it became unprofitable to buy new versions. I spent a few months testing free software such as OpenShot, Shotcut, Kdenlive, etc. I decided to stick with Kdenlive and regretted not starting with it earlier. It would probably have saved me some money. But in this case, many years ago, Kdenlive was not as popular or developed as the paid solutions. The important thing is that I tried. It took time and learning a new program, but thanks to it I am happy to have a few more dollars in my pocket when I edit a video from time to time. This does not change the fact that I make a small donation to Kdenlive every year. Usually it is a third of what I saved by not buying a paid solution.

There are also those who shout what a fool I am for paying for free software… I don’t agree, because I can argue that in many ways. Like thanking someone for their work, for something I can use for free, like supporting a favourite project so it doesn’t die in the near future, etc. Unfortunately, I’ve had the opportunity to have such a conversation with several people, and no matter what argument I gave, I was a fool in their eyes. One person could afford everything, but he was just a cheap bastard, and the other bought an Adobe Premiere Pro licence even though he didn’t know the first thing about editing, but it’s the best and most expensive programme, and when you use it, it’s prestigious. And I’m not saying that these people are idiots. They can do whatever they want, but why is it hurting them so much what I do with my money? Remember that there are people behind free projects who give their time, knowledge and passion. Every dollar they receive as a donation really puts a smile on their faces. Believe me, it’s a great feeling to be able to live from your hobby or project in your basement because people are supporting you with small amounts of money. Whatever.

It’s best to have your own brain, to think logically and choose what’s best for you at any given moment, and to support each other when we share something with others.

What was this article supposed to be about, oh yeah, about cool apps and services that are built to give you more privacy than the competition.

This is a list of apps and services that I recommend from my experience as alternatives to mainstream solutions or those that don’t care about privacy. Don’t use them all at once, pick and choose according to your needs, encourage your loved ones to try them as alternatives to what they’re using now. When you find something you like and use it, don’t forget to support the developer.

Oh! Another example in my case is that, like everyone else, I eventually left WhatsApp (when Facebook bought it), I never had an official Facebook account and I never used this portal for anything other than OSINT and API for the tools I use. Because of my profession (or because I am not a social person, or because I have no friends at all… xD), I never trusted any social networking site and at some point I started to appreciate that messengers encrypt messages. WhatsApp does it now, but at the time I left it, it wasn’t ideal. I left my friends a link to Signal and said that from now on you can write to me there because I won’t be on WhatsApp anymore. And those who wanted to write with me installed Signal (or I’ll be alone forever). And after a while they convinced more and more people around them. They just liked it. Privacy and security was cool. Today it is still cool, but it has also become a marketing ploy. Unfortunately, when I go to Spain or Portugal, WhatsApp is like the official national messenger there. Then I have a local SIM card and a phone just to order a taxi or contact various companies there. One thing I see every year is that taxi drivers, for example, have several applications besides WhatsApp, such as Signal or Telegram, because more and more people, especially tourists, are using an alternative solution. I would like Signal to be the only solution in the future and solutions like Facebook, Messanger or WhatsApp to die a natural death like ICQ, MSN, Gadu-Gadu or AOL. Unfortunately, as I have already mentioned, people do not like change and they have what they have installed because everyone else has installed it at some point or because it is integrated with another service they have always used. And also because the survivors make a lot of money from you continuing to use their solution.

Okay, enough whining. Here are the options I recommend you test out for yourself to make the Internet and tech world a better place in the future. Become an conscious user. Look around and choose alternatives that do not spy on you and respect your privacy.
