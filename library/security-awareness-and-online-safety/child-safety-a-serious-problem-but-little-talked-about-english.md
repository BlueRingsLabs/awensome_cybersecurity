Social Networks and Their Problems
• Social media is a powerful tool for connection and expression, but it
can also be a dangerous environment for children and teens. Issues
such as cyberbullying, exposure to inappropriate content, and
manipulation by malicious adults are common. It is crucial that
parents and guardians are aware of their children’s online activities
and openly discuss the dangers.
Joas A Santos
Social Networks and Their Problems -
Cyberbullying
• Cyberbullying is one of the most prevalent issues on social media,
involving acts of intimidation, humiliation or threats carried out
through digital platforms. Unlike traditional bullying, cyberbullying
can occur at any time of the day or night, and the attacks can be
anonymous and quickly disseminated to a wide audience,
exacerbating the psychological harm to the victim. The persistence of
online posts can also prolong the suffering of the affected person,
making it essential for parents to monitor their children’s online
activities and social interactions.
Joas A Santos
Social Networks and Their Problems – Cyberbullying
#2
• To prevent or protect yourself from cyberbullying, it is essential to take a
proactive approach that includes education about safe online behavior and
the importance of mutual respect. Effective strategies include parents and
guardians regularly monitoring online activity, using privacy settings to
control who can see and interact with social media posts. In cases of
harassment, it is important to document all interactions, block the
perpetrators, and report abuse to the appropriate platforms and
authorities. Additionally, it may be beneficial to seek online reputation
management services to help clean up harmful content and restore your
personal image. When cyberbullying involves serious threats, police
intervention may be necessary to ensure safety and seek justice,
underscoring the importance of treating these situations with the
seriousness they require.

Social Networks and Their Problems - Exposure
to Inappropriate Content
• Social media can expose young people to inappropriate content,
including pornography, violence and hate speech. This exposure can
occur even with the use of safety filters, as algorithms are not always
able to filter out all inappropriate content. Parental supervision and
the use of parental control tools are recommended to help mitigate
this risk.

Social Networks and Their Problems – Online
Predators
• Online predators often use social media to gain the trust of minors.
They may pose as young people to obtain photos, personal
information or to arrange encounters that can lead to abuse.
Educating them about the risks associated with strangers online and
encouraging open communication within the family are key
preventative measures.

Social Networks and Their Problems – Stalking
• Online stalking orcyberstalkinginvolves persistent tracking,
observation and intrusive contact that can be extremely disturbing
and frightening for young people. This can include secretly monitoring
children’s online activities, sending obsessive messages, and even
threats. Children need to be taught how to protect their personal
information and to report any suspicious activity to parents or
authorities.

Social Networks and Their Problems – Identity
Theft
• Children and teens are attractive targets for identity theft because
their clean records can be used fraudulently for years without
detection. Personal information can be obtained through social
engineering or security breaches. It is crucial to teach children never
to share sensitive information online and to use strict privacy settings
on their accounts.

Social Networks and Their Problems – Doxing
• Doxing is the practice of publishing private information about
someone online without their permission, usually with malicious
intent. This can include addresses, phone numbers, emails, or
personal documents. Doxing can lead to harassment and physical
threats. It is crucial that young people understand the importance of
keeping their personal information private and the consequences of
sharing other people’s information.

Identity theft
• Identity theft is a growing threat in the digital age, often exploited to
create fake profiles and commit fraud. This type of crime involves the
appropriation of someone’s personal information for illicit use, and
can have devastating financial and emotional consequences for
victims.
Joas A Santos
Identity Theft – Modus Operandi
• Modus Operandi of Criminals
1.Information Collection: Criminals start by acquiring victims’ personal data. This
can be done in a variety of ways, including phishing attacks via email or text
messages, security breaches in large databases, social engineering, exploiting
social networks to obtain personal details, or even through malware installed on
devices to capture keystrokes or information directly from forms filled out online.
2.Creating Fake Profiles: With this data, fraudsters can create fake profiles on social
networks. These profiles are used to gain credibility and access even more
information, interacting with other people or carrying out activities with the
stolen identity.
3.Sale of Information: In some cases, the stolen details are sold on online black
markets to other criminals, who can then use the information for their own illicit
purposes, increasing the scope of the initial damage.
Joas A Santos
Identity Theft – Preventive Measures
• To combat identity theft, it is crucial to adopt robust security practices,
such as:
• Using Two-Factor Authentication: Whenever possible, enable two-factor
authentication to add an extra layer of security.
• Regular Monitoring: Regularly check bank statements and credit reports
for suspicious activity.
• Safety Education: Educate yourself and family members about the dangers
of sharing personal information and how to recognize phishing attempts.
• Use of Security Software: Install and maintain up-to-date antivirus
software andantimalwareon all devices.
• Password Strengthening: Use strong, unique passwords for different
accounts and services.

Deep Nudes &Sextortion
• Deep Nudesrefers to the creation of fake images, where original photos of a clothed
person are transformed into nudes through artificial intelligence algorithms. These
images can look convincingly real, even though they are completely fabricated. The
technology uses machine learning to estimate what a person’s body would look like
without clothes, and such abuse of AI can cause significant emotional harm.
• Sextortionis a form of extortion that occurs when someone threatens to release intimate
images or private information about a person unless they comply with certain demands,
usually of a sexual or financial nature. Often, the perpetrator may have already obtained
intimate images of the victim through deception or hacking, or may threaten to fabricate
such images using techniques such as deep nudes.
• Briberyinvolves threats to expose information or images that could be embarrassing or
damaging to a person's reputation if certain conditions are not met. This can be
devastating to the mental health of anyone, especially young people.

Digital Games – Problems and Prevention
• Problems:Online gaming provides a breeding ground for a variety of digital risks that affect
children, including the presence of online predators and exposure to excessive spending.
Predators use gaming as a platform to establish relationships with children under the guise of
anonymity, which can lead to dangerous manipulation or even abuse. In addition, the business
model of many games encouragesmicrotransactionsto advance or improve the gaming
experience, which may promote irresponsible spending habits in young people.
• Predator Modus Operandi:Predators in online games often create profiles that appear harmless
or present themselves as young people of the same age as their victims. They initiate friendly
conversations to gain the trust of children, gradually moving the conversations to private
platforms or even to real-world encounters. They usegrooming, where they emotionally
manipulate the child to obtain compromising images, personal information or physical
encounters.
• Prevention:To combat these risks, it is essential for parents to install and configure parental
control software that helps monitor and limit online interactions in games. Regular discussions
about online safety and establishing clear rules about what to share and who to interact with are
essential. Additionally, educating children about the dangers of impulsive spending and setting
limits on in-game purchases are best practices.

Digital Communities – Problems and
Prevention
• Problems:Online communities, while they can serve as spaces for support
and learning, also expose children to inappropriate content and toxic
behavior. Hate speech, cyberbullying, and the promotion of risky behavior
are common on many platforms. Such exposures can negatively impact
children’s social and emotional development, leading to self-esteem and
mental health issues.
• Prevention:It is crucial that parents actively participate in their children’s
digital lives, understanding the platforms they use and the communities
they are part of. Moderation tools and content filters should be applied
whenever possible. Additionally, creating an open environment for
discussions about online experiences allows children to feel comfortable
talking about anything that makes them uncomfortable or feels
inappropriate.

Poorly trained tutors – Problems and
Prevention
• Problems:A lack of adequate preparation by guardians in
understanding and navigating the digital environment can lead to
ineffective supervision of children’s online activities. This can result in
overly intrusive surveillance that alienates the child, or negligent
supervision that leaves the child vulnerable to online threats.
• Prevention:Guardians, including parents and teachers, should seek
out digital safety training through courses, workshops, or educational
materials available on trusted platforms. This education should
include techniques for effectively and respectfully monitoring
children’s online activities, understanding both the dangers and best
practices for promoting a safe digital environment.
